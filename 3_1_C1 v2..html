Adding Two Fractions: Parallel Presentation (Numerical vs. Algebraic)

📊 Slide 1: Fractions with Like Denominators
Level 1
🎯 Objective: When the bottom numbers are the same, add the top numbers and keep the bottom number the same[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

27+37
  • The bottom numbers are the same (7)[cite: 1].
  • Add the top numbers directly: 2+37[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 57

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

ac+bc
  • The bottom numbers are the same (c)[cite: 1].
  • Add the top numbers directly: a+bc[cite: 1]
Final Answer: a+bc
💡 Golden Rule: The bottom number acts like a label[cite: 1]. You can only add the top numbers when the labels match[cite: 1]!
📊 Slide 2: Fractions with Different Denominators
Level 2
🎯 Objective: Find a common bottom number by multiplying the bottom numbers together[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

13+25
  • Common bottom number is 3×5=15[cite: 1].
  • Change fractions: 1×515+2×315[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: 5+615[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 1115

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1a+1b
  • Common bottom number is a×b=ab[cite: 1].
  • Change fractions: bab+aab[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: b+aab[cite: 1]
Final Answer: a+bab
💡 Golden Rule: When bottom numbers share no factors, multiply them together to make a common bottom number[cite: 1].
📊 Slide 3: General Fractions with Variable Numerators
Level 3
🎯 Objective: Use cross-multiplication to add any two fractions[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

23+45
  • Common bottom number is 3×5=15[cite: 1].
  • Cross-multiply: 2×515+4×315[cite: 1]
  • Add products: 10+1215[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 2215

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

xa+yb
  • Common bottom number is ab[cite: 1].
  • Cross-multiply: x×bab+y×aab[cite: 1]
  • Add terms: xb+yaab[cite: 1]
Final Answer: bx+ayab
💡 Golden Rule: The general rule for adding fractions is: AB+CD=AD+BCBD[cite: 1].
📊 Slide 4: Polynomial Numerators (Expanding Brackets)
Level 4
🎯 Objective: Put brackets around top expressions and expand them after making a common denominator[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

x+12+x−33
  • Common bottom number is 2×3=6[cite: 1].
  • Set up fractions: 3(x+1)6+2(x−3)6[cite: 1]
  • Expand brackets: 3x+3+2x−66[cite: 1]
  • Combine like terms: 5x−36[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 5x−36

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

x+ab+x−ba
  • Common bottom number is ab[cite: 1].
  • Set up fractions: a(x+a)ab+b(x−b)ab[cite: 1]
  • Expand brackets: ax+a2+bx−b2ab[cite: 1]
  • Group terms: ax+bx+a2−b2ab[cite: 1]
Final Answer: x(a+b)+a2−b2ab
💡 Golden Rule: If the top part has more than one term, put it inside brackets before cross-multiplying[cite: 1]!
📊 Slide 5: Adding a Whole Number to a Fraction
Level 5
🎯 Objective: Turn a whole number or single term into a fraction by placing it over 1[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

3+25
  • Write 3 as 31; common bottom number is 5[cite: 1].
  • Change fraction: 3×55+25=155+25[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: 15+25[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 175

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

x+ab
  • Write x as x1; common bottom number is b[cite: 1].
  • Change fraction: x×bb+ab=bxb+ab[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: 15+25[cite: 1]
Final Answer: bx+ab
💡 Golden Rule: You can write any whole term as a fraction by putting 1 under it[cite: 1].
📊 Slide 6: Denominators with Variables and Common Factors
Level 6
🎯 Objective: Find the Lowest Common Denominator (LCD) instead of multiplying the bottom numbers[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

2x+32x
  • The Lowest Common Denominator (LCD) is 2x[cite: 1].
  • Multiply first fraction top and bottom by 2: 42x+32x[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: 4+32x[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 72x

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

ax+bcx
  • The Lowest Common Denominator (LCD) is cx[cite: 1].
  • Multiply first fraction top and bottom by c: accx+bcx[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: ac+bcx[cite: 1]
Final Answer: ac+bcx
💡 Golden Rule: Always check if one bottom number fits into the other before multiplying them together[cite: 1].
📊 Slide 7: Binomial Denominators
Level 7
🎯 Objective: Treat expressions like (x + a) as single whole units[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

1x+1+2x+3
  • Common bottom number is (x+1)(x+3)[cite: 1].
  • Cross-multiply: 1(x+3)(x+1)(x+3)+2(x+1)(x+1)(x+3)[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: x+3+2x+2(x+1)(x+3)[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 3x+5x2+4x+3

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1x+a+1x+b
  • Common bottom number is (x+a)(x+b)[cite: 1].
  • Cross-multiply: x+b(x+a)(x+b)+x+a(x+a)(x+b)[cite: 1]
  • Add top numbers: x+b+x+a(x+a)(x+b)[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 2x+a+bx2+(a+b)x+ab
💡 Golden Rule: Keep two-term expressions wrapped in brackets so you don't break them apart[cite: 1].
📊 Slide 8: Opposite Factors in Denominators
Level 8
🎯 Objective: Spot opposite terms like (x - y) and (y - x), then factor out -1 to simplify[cite: 1].

🟢 Numerical Fractions

Calculate:

5x−2+32−x
  • Note that (2−x)=−(x−2)[cite: 1].
  • Factor out -1: 32−x=−3x−2[cite: 1]
  • Rewrite: 5x−2−3x−2[cite: 1]
  • Subtract top numbers: 5−3x−2[cite: 1]
Final Answer: 2x−2

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

ax−y+by−x
  • Note that (y−x)=−(x−y)[cite: 1].
  • Factor out -1: by−x=−bx−y[cite: 1]
  • Rewrite: ax−y−bx−y[cite: 1]
  • Subtract top numbers: a−bx−y[cite: 1]
Final Answer: a−bx−y
💡 Golden Rule: Remember that (b−a) is equal to −(a−b)[cite: 1]. Factoring out -1 makes finding common bottom numbers easier[cite: 1]!
📊 Slide 9: Shared Linear Factors
Level 9
🎯 Objective: Find the common linear factor in a(mx - b) and c(mx - b) to get the LCD.

🟢 Numerical Coefficients Example

Calculate (for x≠12):

12(2x−1)+53(2x−1)
  • Identify shared factor (2x - 1) and numbers 2 and 3.
  • The LCD is 2×3×(2x−1)=6(2x−1).
  • Adjust top numbers: 36(2x−1)+106(2x−1)
  • Add top numbers: 3+106(2x−1)
Final Answer: 136(2x−1)

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1a(mx−b)+1c(mx−b)
  • Identify shared factor (mx - b) and numbers a and c.
  • The LCD is ac(mx−b).
  • Adjust top numbers: cac(mx−b)+aac(mx−b)
  • Add top numbers: c+aac(mx−b)
Final Answer: a+cac(mx−b)
💡 Golden Rule: Keep shared factors unchanged and only find the lowest common multiple of the outside numbers.
📊 Slide 10: Difference of Two Squares & Linear Denominators
Level 10
🎯 Objective: Factor (x² - a²) into (x - a)(x + a) to find the Lowest Common Denominator.

🟢 Numerical Factors Example

Calculate (for x≠±3):

1x+3+2x2−9
  • Factor second bottom term: (x2−9)=(x−3)(x+3).
  • The LCD is (x−3)(x+3).
  • Multiply first fraction top and bottom by (x - 3): 1(x−3)(x−3)(x+3)+2(x−3)(x+3)
  • Combine top numbers: x−3+2(x−3)(x+3)
Final Answer: x−1x2−9

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1x+a+bx2−a2
  • Factor second bottom term: (x2−a2)=(x−a)(x+a).
  • The LCD is (x−a)(x+a).
  • Multiply first fraction top and bottom by (x - a): 1(x−a)(x−a)(x+a)+b(x−a)(x+a)
  • Combine top numbers: x−a+b(x−a)(x+a)
Final Answer: x−a+bx2−a2
💡 Golden Rule: The difference of two squares (x² - a²) already contains the factor (x + a).
📊 Slide 11: Difference of Squares (a²x² - b²) with Positive Linear (max + mb)
Level 11
🎯 Objective: Factor both (a²x² - b²) = (ax - b)(ax + b) and (max + mb) = m(ax + b) to find the LCD.

🟢 Numerical Factors Example

Calculate (for x≠±32):

14x2−9+26x+9
  • Factor bottom terms: (4x2−9)=(2x−3)(2x+3) and (6x+9)=3(2x+3).
  • The LCD is 3(2x−3)(2x+3).
  • Adjust top numbers: 33(2x−3)(2x+3)+2(2x−3)3(2x−3)(2x+3)
  • Expand and add: 3+4x−63(2x−3)(2x+3)
Final Answer: 4x−33(4x2−9)

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1a2x2−b2+1max+mb
  • Factor bottom terms: (ax−b)(ax+b) and m(ax+b).
  • The LCD is m(ax−b)(ax+b).
  • Adjust top numbers: mm(ax−b)(ax+b)+ax−bm(ax−b)(ax+b)
  • Combine top numbers: ax−b+mm(a2x2−b2)
Final Answer: ax−b+mm(a2x2−b2)
💡 Golden Rule: Factor out both the difference of squares and common numbers first to find the shared factor (ax + b).
📊 Slide 12: Difference of Squares (a²x² - b²) with Negative Linear (max - mb)
Level 12
🎯 Objective: Factor both (a²x² - b²) = (ax - b)(ax + b) and (max - mb) = m(ax - b) to find the LCD.

🟢 Numerical Factors Example

Calculate (for x≠±53):

29x2−25+16x−10
  • Factor bottom terms: (9x2−25)=(3x−5)(3x+5) and (6x−10)=2(3x−5).
  • The LCD is 2(3x−5)(3x+5).
  • Adjust top numbers: 2(2)2(3x−5)(3x+5)+1(3x+5)2(3x−5)(3x+5)
  • Expand and add: 4+3x+52(9x2−25)
Final Answer: 3x+92(9x2−25)

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1a2x2−b2+1max−mb
  • Factor bottom terms: (ax−b)(ax+b) and m(ax−b).
  • The LCD is m(ax−b)(ax+b).
  • Adjust top numbers: mm(ax−b)(ax+b)+ax+bm(ax−b)(ax+b)
  • Combine top numbers: ax+b+mm(a2x2−b2)
Final Answer: ax+b+mm(a2x2−b2)
💡 Golden Rule: Match (ax - b) with (max - mb) by factoring out m. The common factor (ax - b) appears once in the LCD.
📊 Slide 13: Perfect Squares (ax ± b)² with Scaled Linear Denominators
Level 13
🎯 Objective: Factor perfect squares (a²x² ± 2abx + b²) = (ax ± b)² and find the LCD when combined with m(ax ± b).

🟢 Numerical Factors Example

Calculate (for x≠23):

19x2−12x+4+26x−4
  • Factor bottom terms: (9x2−12x+4)=(3x−2)2 and (6x−4)=2(3x−2).
  • The LCD is 2(3x−2)2.
  • Adjust top numbers: 1(2)2(3x−2)2+2(3x−2)2(3x−2)2
  • Expand and add: 2+6x−42(3x−2)2=6x−22(3x−2)2
  • Simplify by dividing factor of 2: 2(3x−1)2(3x−2)2
Final Answer: 3x−1(3x−2)2

🔵 Algebraic Fractions

Simplify:

1(ax+b)2+1max+mb
  • Factor bottom terms: (ax+b)2 and m(ax+b).
  • The LCD takes the highest power of the common factor: m(ax+b)2.
  • Adjust top numbers: mm(ax+b)2+ax+bm(ax+b)2
  • Combine top numbers: ax+b+mm(ax+b)2
Final Answer: ax+b+mm(ax+b)2
💡 Golden Rule: For repeated factors like (ax + b)², always use the highest power when making the Lowest Common Denominator.
